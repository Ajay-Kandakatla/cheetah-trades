Chart Maps → **📰 News**, right after 🔥 Hottest. One composed read,
`GET /chart-maps/news` (no parameters), four blocks, each failing on its own.

## The four blocks

| block | source | what it prints |
|---|---|---|
| **Market read** | the Market Gauge (`/market/gauge`) | the gauge's own state as a word, daily **and** weekly |
| **📅 Macro** | `macro_calendar.get_macro_calendar()` | T1 market movers + T2 trend shapers, next 14 days, next T1 called out |
| **Sectors vs RSP** | the 🔥 Hottest board (`/rotation/hottest`) | every sector: bullish/bearish on day/week/month, 🔥/🧊 heat, 📰 bull + bear case, 🗺️ heatmap |
| **📰 Headlines** | `news_search/core.py` — the ONE news routine | last 36 h, keyword `stock market OR Nasdaq OR S&P 500` |

**One budget.** Every leg runs under `asyncio.wait_for(…, LEG_BUDGET_SEC = 8)`.
A leg past it serves `ok: false` with a "still loading — refresh" reason and the
other three still draw. `wait_for` on a thread abandons, never kills, so a slow
leg keeps warming its cache for the next refresh.

## The market word — the gauge's, mapped once

```
MARKET_WORD = {"constructive": "bullish", "caution": "mixed", "risk_off": "bearish"}
```

in `backend/chart_maps/news_tab.py`, and nowhere else: a contract fails if the
browser names a gauge state in a quoted literal, or if another file under
`backend/chart_maps/` does. **No new score, no new threshold** — the word is a
translation of the gauge's own state, printed beside the gauge's label and
score. **Daily and weekly both show**, because they disagree (the day this
shipped: daily **mixed · Caution 60**, weekly **bullish · Constructive 84**), and
the tab says so in words.

**"mixed" for Caution is my word, not his** — he asked for bullish or bearish.
His call.

## The sector table — two yardsticks, each named

* **bullish / bearish** on each leg (day · 5 days · 21 days) = the sign of the
  sector's median member against **RSP**, the equal-weight benchmark. A
  description of what **already moved**, not a forecast. Zero reads `flat`.
* **🔥 / 🧊 heat** = `rotation.heat` — the pooled tercile of `rel_5d` across
  industries, sectors and themes, the same word the demand tiles print.

A sector can read **bullish vs RSP and 🧊 cold at once** (up a little, while most
groups ran harder). Both show, each column naming its yardstick. His call
whether to keep both.

**"🔥 hot, lagging today"** is one click — `hot_lagging_1d` = heat is hot AND
the day leg is below RSP. That is his "hot sectors that are bearish".
**"🧊 cold, leading today"** is the mirror.

**"today" only when `d1.live`.** Outside a live session the day leg is the last
close, and the header, the chips and a muted line under the table say so. The
word "today" is never taken from the clock.

The **📰** column reuses the 🔥 Hottest day-tag chip: a model-written bull case
**and** bear case where one exists, never a score (see
`rotation/sector_news_tags.py`). Empty on the day this shipped — the tag cron
had written nothing for 3 days while both LLM providers were down; both were
healthy again by the afternoon.

## Said plainly, on the tab

Sector heat was **measured** 2026-09-09 on the 21-day definition, 50,191
demand-zone arrivals: it predicted nothing — **−0.57pp on win rate, 95% CI
−1.87 to +0.71**. "A cold sector just sits there" measured **inverted** — hot
minus cold at 5 sessions **−2.55pp, 95% CI −4.48 to −0.65** (cold turned
faster). The **5-session** heat that decides the 🔥/🧊 word here is
**UNMEASURED**; the 21-day null neither validates nor condemns it.

Nothing on this tab gates a scan, pushes a phone, sizes a position or enters a
lane.

## Fixed on the way — the double-counted jobless claims

"Jobless claims" printed on Thursday **and** Friday every week, and a phantom
"Retail sales" sat on 10-08. Cause: FRED schedules **shadow releases** whose
names contain a tier needle — e.g. release 469 "State Unemployment Insurance
Weekly Claims Report" lands on Fridays and matched as the Thursday print (180).
The `(kind, date)` dedupe only hid a shadow landing on the print's own date.
Eight shadow names across five kinds are now excluded before the needle match;
full table with FRED release ids in `docs/sepa/macro_event_overlay.md`. Also: a
lock on the calendar's cold fetch, so two cold callers share one FRED pull.

Verified on live FRED data through the branch API: **one claims row per week,
Thursdays only** (09-24, 10-01, 10-08), no retail row.
